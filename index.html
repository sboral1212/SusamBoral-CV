Profile

I am a Research Fellow at Trinity College Dublin, holding a PhD in Applied Mathematics with a specialisation in Ocean Engineering, and have over three years of postdoctoral research experience. My work develops along two parallel research directions: quantum computing algorithms for differential equations, and mathematical and numerical modelling of marine engineering systems.

In quantum computing, I investigate Kacewicz-based numerical integration, hybrid quantum–classical methods and their application to nonlinear differential equations and partial differential equations (PDEs). In marine engineering, I develop and validate models for hydroelastic waves, fluid–structure interactions and the dynamics of submarine power cables.

I aim to bring these areas together by investigating how quantum and hybrid computational techniques can be used to simulate demanding marine engineering problems, while benchmarking their accuracy and practical cost against established classical methods.

Research Direction: Connecting Quantum & Marine Simulation

My longer-term research objective is to explore whether quantum algorithms and hybrid quantum–classical workflows can support marine-engineering simulations involving nonlinear waves, fluid–structure interaction and dynamic offshore cables. This is an active research direction, not a claim of demonstrated quantum advantage: careful benchmarking against reliable classical solvers will be central to the work.
